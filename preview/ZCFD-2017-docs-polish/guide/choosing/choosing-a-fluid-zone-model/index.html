Choosing a fluid-zone model

Which fluid zone represents the device, obstruction or motion in the flow?

A fluid zone is a region of cells in which the solver applies a source term or a reference frame. Each zone is a named block in the model's fluid_zones. Its type sets what is applied. The cells are selected by zones, a list of the mesh's cell zones, or by definition, a closed surface.

Choose the zone type by what the flow must feel. A rotor, propeller or turbine whose blades the mesh does not resolve is an actuator disc. A distributed resistance, such as a radiator, a fence or a packed bed, is a porous zone, and a tree canopy over terrain is a canopy zone. Any other body force is a momentum source, written as a Python function. A part of a still mesh that turns, such as a wheel or an impeller, is a rotating zone: its cells are solved in a rotating frame. A frame over the whole mesh, a translating frame, and a mesh that moves are not fluid zones; they are the motion in the model's transforms, described in Placing and moving a mesh.

typeRepresentsSelects its cells by
rotatinga rotating frame over part of a still meshcell zones
discan actuator disc: a rotor, propeller or turbineits geometry and definition
porousa porous medium, as viscous and inertial resistancecell zones, definition, or the whole mesh
canopya tree canopy in a terrain wind modelcell zones, definition, field, or the whole mesh
momentuman arbitrary body force, from a Python functioncell zones, definition, or the whole mesh
rbf transform, idw transforma surface deformation carried into the volume meshzones, the moving boundary faces
ablvalidated, but has no effect on the solutionthe whole mesh

Fluid zones are a dictionary of named blocks, keyed in the same way as boundary conditions:

{
    'model': {
        '<mesh_name>': {
            'mesh': '<mesh_name>.h5',
            'fluid_zones': {
                'FZ_1': {'type': '...', ...},
                'FZ_2': {'type': '...', ...},
            },
            'boundary_conditions': {...},
        }
    }
}

Each block's type selects the kind of zone, and determines which other keys are valid and required in that block. An unrecognised key anywhere in a fluid zone is rejected at validation, not silently ignored.

Selecting cells

A fluid zone that acts on a volume selects its cells in one of three ways:

  • cell zones, a list of the mesh's cell zone numbers. Every cell labelled with one of those numbers belongs to the fluid zone. This suits a region that was meshed as a body of its own, such as a wheel, a fan or a radiator core.
  • definition, the path to a closed-surface VTP file. The cells the surface encloses belong to the fluid zone. This suits a region the mesh does not mark, or one drawn after the mesh was made.
  • neither, in which case the fluid zone covers the whole mesh.

A fluid zone takes at most one of these. Which it may take depends on its type:

typecell zonesdefinitionneither
rotatingrequirednot acceptednot accepted
momentum, porousacceptedacceptedthe whole mesh
canopyacceptedacceptedthe whole mesh
discnot acceptedrequirednot accepted
ablnot acceptednot acceptedalways the whole mesh

A canopy zone may instead give field, a canopy height map, which selects the cells beneath the canopy height; it takes only one of cell zones, definition and field. A disc zone places its forces with its geometry and selects the cells that receive them with its definition.

"fluid zones": {
    "fan": {"type": "momentum", "cell zones": [4], "func": fan_force},
    "screen": {"type": "porous", "definition": "screen.vtp", "alpha": 1.0e-7},
    "drive": {"type": "momentum", "func": pressure_gradient},
},

Cell zone numbers belong to the cells of the mesh, and are separate from the boundary zone numbers in a boundary condition's zones. The two may share a number without any connection between them. See Finding cell zone numbers for how to read them from a mesh.

The zones of an rbf transform or idw transform zone are boundary zones: the moving surfaces whose deformation is carried into the volume mesh, numbered as for boundary conditions.

Fluid zones in the incompressible solver

All the source-term zone types, momentum, porous, canopy, disc and abl, are available to the incompressible solver, {"solver_settings": {"type": "incompressible"}}, as well as to the compressible one. Three points differ.

The incompressible momentum equation is solved per unit mass, with the density divided out, so a momentum zone's func must return a force per unit mass rather than per unit volume. The porous, canopy and actuator disc models handle this internally, so their inputs (alpha, C2, cd, and the disc's geometry and model dictionaries) are the same for both solvers.

A strong porous or canopy zone under the segregated SIMPLE scheme, {"convergence_control": {"scheme": {"name": "simple"}}}, needs the scheme's pressure_regularisation set. Their drag adds to the momentum diagonal, so in a heavily blocked cell every pressure-correction face coefficient collapses towards zero, and that cell's row of the p′ Laplacian becomes nearly empty. The conjugate-gradient solver then reports an indefinite matrix, and the run diverges. A value around 1e-2 restores stability, though a near-solid block still converges more slowly and less far than under the coupled scheme; for strong drag, prefer the 'coupled' scheme. Weak drag, such as a typical canopy, converges under either scheme with the default of 0, and the two agree to discretisation level. momentum, disc and abl zones are explicit body forces that never touch the momentum diagonal, and are unaffected either way.

The canopy turbulence source is defined only for the sst model. Under any other turbulence model the canopy applies its momentum sink alone, and the solver logs a warning.

Actuator discs

A wind turbine or a propeller affects the flow, but it is often impractical to resolve the flow down to the scale of its blades. An actuator disc represents the device instead, as a set of source terms superimposed on the flow.

Turbines and propellers behave almost identically in the actuator disc model; the differences lie only in the coordinate systems and sign conventions usually used to define them. In zCFD an actuator disc is a fluid zone of type 'disc'.

The type key of the disc's model block selects a 'simple' disc, with fixed or curve-driven thrust and power coefficients, or a 'bet' disc, based on blade-element theory.

The normal sets the direction of the disc's force. In zCFD the normal points in the direction of the force on the fluid: for a propeller it points with the flow, and for a turbine it points towards the incoming flow.

A disc zone is built from four dictionaries and a few keys of its own:

KeyHolds
controllerhow the disc's forces are controlled: fixed, with omega and pitch, or tsr curve, with a tip speed ratio curve
geometrycentre, normal, up, inner radius and outer radius

modelthe force model: simple, averaged over the whole disc, or bet, by blade elements

reference_plane and reference_pointfor a simple model, where the reference velocity is taken: on a reference plane when reference_plane is true, otherwise at reference_point, or from the solver's reference condition if neither is set
update_frequencyhow often, in cycles, the disc's source is re-sampled
rotation_directionthe direction of rotation, such as clockwise

Note

Keys may be written with spaces, 'inner radius' and 'tip speed ratio', or with underscores, inner_radius and tip_speed_ratio. Both forms are normalised to the same field before validation.

Rotating zones

A rotating zone solves the flow in part of a mesh in a frame that turns about an axis, while the mesh itself stays still. It is the multiple reference frame (MRF) treatment: the cells of the zone carry the Coriolis and centrifugal sources of the rotating frame, and the cells outside it are solved in the stationary frame. It suits a body that turns steadily inside a stationary one, such as a road wheel under a car, a fan in a duct or the impeller of a compressor, where a steady run is to give the time-averaged flow.

KeyValueMeaning
cell zonesa list of cell zone numbersthe cells solved in the rotating frame
axisa vectorthe axis of rotation, in placed coordinates
origina pointa point on the axis, in placed coordinates
omegarad/sthe rate of rotation, positive right-handed about axis

A rotating zone is always a frame on a still mesh. It takes no definition, no moving mesh and no ramp, and it runs at its full rate from the first cycle. A cell zone may belong to only one rotating zone, but a model may carry several rotating zones, each with its own axis and rate.

A rotating zone or a motion

Both a rotating zone and the motion in the model's transforms can solve flow in a rotating frame. They differ in how much of the mesh the frame covers, and in whether the mesh may move:

The meshWrite
a still mesh, part of which turnsa rotating fluid zone on the cell zones that turn
a still mesh that turns as a whole, such as a rotor in its own meshtransforms > motion with "moving mesh": False
a still mesh in a translating frametransforms > motion of type translating, with "moving mesh": False
a mesh that moves through the runtransforms > motion with "moving mesh": True, and an overset or sliding interface

A model takes one source of frame: a model with a rotating zone carries no motion, and a model with a motion carries no rotating zone. A mesh with a sliding or overset interface turns as a whole, so it takes a motion and no rotating zone; see Placing and moving a mesh. A rotating zone avoids that interface: the turning and stationary cells stay in one mesh, joined by ordinary interior faces.

Preparing the mesh

The region that turns must be meshed as one or more cell zones of its own, enclosing the body that turns and nothing that does not. The surface between those cells and the stationary ones carries the change of frame, so it should:

  • be a surface of revolution about the axis, such as a cylinder or a disc, so that the flow crossing it is close to uniform around the axis;
  • lie clear of the blades or spokes, with a few cells between the body and the surface;
  • be made of faces shared by the cells either side, with no boundary zone or interface on it.

A rotating cell zone that cuts through a stationary wall, or a surface that wraps closely around blades, places the change of frame where the flow varies most around the axis, and the solution then depends on where the surface was drawn.

A wall bounding the cells of a rotating zone turns with the frame, so the blades and hub of a body inside the zone need no velocity of their own. A wall velocity takes precedence over the frame: a stationary wall inside the zone, such as a shroud, carries "velocity": {"type": "linear", "vector": [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]}, and a surface that turns but lies outside the zone, such as the tread of a tyre, carries a rotating wall velocity with the same axis, origin and rate as the zone.

Finding cell zone numbers

A zCFD mesh labels each cell with a cell zone number, held in its cellZone dataset. The Fluent and Fluent CFF converters keep the cell zone numbers of the Fluent file, and the zone helper they write beside the mesh maps each Fluent cell zone name to its number in fluid_zone; see Zone numbers:

from wheel_zone import fluid_zone

parameters["model"]["wheel"]["fluid zones"] = {
    "wheel": {"type": "rotating", "cell zones": [fluid_zone["wheel"]], ...},
}

The helper may not list every cell zone in the mesh. For any mesh, the numbers can be read from a solution: add cellzone to the volume output variables and run a few cycles:

"output settings": {"solution": {"volume variables": ["cellzone", "V", "p"]}},

Open the volume output in ParaView and colour it by cellzone. A clip or slice through the turning body shows the number of the region around it; a Threshold filter on cellzone isolates one region, which confirms that it encloses the turning body and no more.

Example: a road wheel

A wheel turning on a road, with the car's speed as the oncoming flow and the floor moving with it. The wheel's radius is 0.2635 m, so at 160 km/h it turns at 168.6 rad/s. The cells around the wheel are cell zone 17, and the tyre's tread and sidewall are boundary zones 14 and 20:

speed = 160.0 / 3.6
wheel_radius = 0.52709 / 2.0
rot_rate = speed / wheel_radius
wheel_axis = [0.0, -0.9988, 0.0498]
wheel_origin = [0.0, -0.6593, 0.2533]

parameters["model"]["wheel"] = {
    "mesh": "wheel.h5",
    "fluid zones": {
        "wheel": {
            "type": "rotating",
            "cell zones": [17],
            "axis": wheel_axis,
            "origin": wheel_origin,
            "omega": rot_rate,
        },
    },
    "boundary conditions": {
        "floor": {
            "zones": [8],
            "type": "wall",
            "kind": "wall function",
            "velocity": {"type": "linear", "vector": [speed, 0.0, 0.0]},
        },
        "tyre": {
            "zones": [14, 20],
            "type": "wall",
            "kind": "wall function",
            "velocity": {
                "type": "rotating",
                "axis": wheel_axis,
                "origin": wheel_origin,
                "omega": rot_rate,
            },
        },
        "hub": {"zones": [10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 19], "type": "wall", "kind": "wall function"},
        "symmetry": {"zones": [9], "type": "symmetry"},
        "farfield": {"zones": [4, 5, 6, 7], "type": "farfield", "condition": "IC_1", "kind": "riemann"},
    },
}

The axis is tilted slightly from the y axis, to follow the camber of the wheel, and the origin lies on the axle. The frame carries the flow through the spokes and the rim; the tyre's walls carry the same rotation, and the floor moves at the oncoming speed, so the contact patch is at rest relative to the road.

Example: a turbocharger compressor

A turbocharger compressor wheel turning at 5,000 rad/s about an axis along −x-x−x, in still air. The Fluent mesh has two cell zones: cell zone 1, named fluid, is the passage around the wheel, and cell zone 0 is the stationary inlet and housing around it. The frame covers cell zone 1, and the inlet and outlet hold IC_2, a reference condition at rest:

import turbo_zone as tz

impeller_axis = [-1.0, 0.0, 0.0]
impeller_origin = [0.0, 0.005967, 0.37498]
impeller_rate = 5000.0

parameters["model"]["turbo"] = {
    "mesh": "turbo.h5",
    "fluid zones": {
        "impeller": {
            "type": "rotating",
            "cell zones": [tz.fluid_zone["fluid"]],
            "axis": impeller_axis,
            "origin": impeller_origin,
            "omega": impeller_rate,
        },
    },
    "boundary conditions": {
        "wheel": {
            "zones": tz.to_id(["comp", "comp-fine", "comp-fe", "comp-extra-fine"]),
            "type": "wall",
            "kind": "wall function",
            "velocity": {
                "type": "rotating",
                "axis": impeller_axis,
                "origin": impeller_origin,
                "omega": impeller_rate,
            },
        },
        "housing": {
            "zones": tz.to_id(["body", "body-fine"]),
            "type": "wall",
            "kind": "wall function",
            "velocity": {"type": "linear", "vector": [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]},
        },
        "ports": {
            "zones": tz.to_id(["vel-inlet", "pressure-outlet"]),
            "type": "farfield",
            "condition": "IC_2",
            "kind": "pressure",
        },
    },
}

The boundary between the cell zones does not follow the walls exactly. Part of the wheel's surface lies against stationary cells, and part of the housing lies against rotating ones, so each wall states its own velocity: the wheel turns with the frame wherever it lies, and the housing stays still where it passes through the rotating passage. Where the zones and walls are uncertain, a velocity on every wall that touches a rotating zone removes the doubt. A radial pressure gradient outlet on a diffuser exit takes its axis from the frame of the cells beside it; see Outflow.

Momentum Source

A momentum source applies an arbitrary body force, supplied as a Python function of the cell centres. The momentum source function returns one source vector per cell, in non-dimensional units. This is a force per unit volume in the compressible solver and a force per unit mass in the incompressible solver.

func is sampled once, when the zone is set up, and is not re-evaluated during the solve; an actuator disc, by contrast, re-samples its source at every update_frequency. kind and frequency are accepted by the schema but have no effect on a momentum zone.

def momentum_drive(*, locations):
    return [(0.0, 0.0, body_force) for _ in locations]

{
    'fluid_zones': {
        'FZ_1': {
            'type': 'momentum',
            'definition': 'pipe_periodic_region.vtp',
            'func': momentum_drive,
        }
    }
}

Canopy

A canopy zone represents a tree canopy in a terrain wind model.

cd, beta_p, beta_d, Ceps_4 and Ceps_5 are defined in the literature, for example Desmond (2014).

def lad_function(*, locations, wall_distances):
    lai = 5.0
    h_can = 20.0
    return [np.interp(d, a_z[0] * h_can, a_z[1]) * lai / h_can for d in wall_distances]

{
    'fluid_zones': {
        'canopy_zone': {
            'type': 'canopy',
            'definition': 'canopy_dacosta.vtp',
            'function': lad_function,
            'cd': 0.25,
            'beta_p': 0.17,
            'beta_d': 3.37,
            'Ceps_4': 0.9,
            'Ceps_5': 0.9,
        },
    }
}

The leaf area density varies with height:

a_z = (
    np.asarray([0.0, 0.43, 0.1, 0.45, 0.2, 0.56, 0.3, 0.74, 0.4, 1.10,
                0.5, 1.35, 0.6, 1.48, 0.7, 1.47, 0.8, 1.35, 0.9, 1.01,
                1.0, 0.00]).reshape(11, 2).T
)
Note

When field is used instead of definition, the height of the canopy at each cell is found from the point of the supplied VTK file nearest the cell centre, and read from a node-based scalar array named 'Height'. A file without that array is rejected at validation.

Atmospheric Boundary Layer (ABL)

Warning

An ABL zone has no physical effect on the solution. Its fields are validated but not read by the solver: the zone reduces to a bare momentum-source kernel over the selected cells, with no source derived from roughness_length, friction_velocity, reference_height or wind_direction. An atmospheric boundary layer profile is set on a reference condition instead; see ABL.

Moving or deforming a mesh

zCFD has four distinct mechanisms for placing, moving or deforming a mesh, or the frame its flow is solved in, each configured per model, that is per mesh:

MechanismWhat it doesWhere it is set
Initial placementa single static rotation, translation or scaling of the whole mesh, applied once when it loadsthe scale and transform in the model's transforms
Rigid motion of a whole mesha mesh rotating or translating during the run, such as a rotor or a moving overset componentthe motion in the model's transforms, with "moving mesh": True; see Placing and moving a mesh
Moving reference framecells of a still mesh solved in a rotating or translating framefor part of the mesh, a rotating fluid zone on its cell zones; see Rotating zones. For the whole mesh, the motion in the model's transforms, with "moving mesh": False
Mesh morphinga known deformation of a surface, such as a wing or blade deflecting under load, carried into the volume mesh by RBF or IDW interpolationan 'rbf transform' or 'idw transform' entry in fluid_zones; see Fluid–structure interaction
