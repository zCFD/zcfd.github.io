Choosing a limiter

Which slope limiter should bound the second-order reconstruction of the compressible solver?

Keep the default, van albada, for general work. Where the reconstructed face values must stay within the range of the neighbouring cells, as they must across a strong shock, use michalak gooch: it is bound preserving, smooth enough not to stall the residual, and switches itself off in smooth flow so that second-order accuracy survives mesh refinement. Use none only for smooth flow with no shocks or strong gradients.

The choice is made with limiter in numerical_scheme:

{ "numerical_scheme": { "order": "second", "limiter": "michalak gooch" } }

The limiter acts on the second-order reconstruction only. It has no effect when order is "first", nor during the cycles covered by first_order_cycles. It belongs to the compressible solver; the incompressible solver limits its gradients through gradient_limiter_type and gradient_limiter in equations instead.

One-dimensional limiters

van albada, van leer and symmetric ratio are functions ψ(a,b)\psi(a, b)ψ(a,b) of two directional differences: aaa is the jump across the face and bbb a backward difference reconstructed from the cell gradient. Each is applied independently to every reconstructed variable and to each side of every face, and ψ\psiψ scales the whole reconstruction increment on that side.

They are regularised by a threshold

ε=0.001⋅12(uL+uR)+10−8\varepsilon = 0.001 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}(u_L + u_R) + 10^{-8}ε=0.001⋅21​(uL​+uR​)+10−8

which is proportional to the magnitude of the variable itself; symmetric ratio uses its absolute value. For a variable that changes sign, a velocity component for example, ε\varepsilonε becomes small where the variable passes through zero, and the limiter then acts on correspondingly smaller variations.

Cell-based limiters

barth jespersen, michalak gooch and venkatakrishnan are multidimensional. They bound the reconstructed face value by the range of values over the cell's whole set of face neighbours, which the one-dimensional limiters do not. They are evaluated per cell, in a pass of their own, and the reconstruction then reads the result.

A kink makes the limiter value flicker between cycles, which can hold the residual at a noise floor. That is the case against barth jespersen, and the reason michalak gooch is preferred.

Switching off in smooth flow

michalak gooch and venkatakrishnan blend ϕ\phiϕ towards 1 wherever the flow is smooth. A cell counts as smooth when the range umax⁡−umin⁡u_{\max} - u_{\min}umax​−umin​ over its neighbours, normalised by the reference state, is small against the threshold

ε2=(KhLref)3\varepsilon^2 = \left(\frac{K h}{L_{ref}}\right)^3ε2=(Lref​Kh​)3

where KKK is limiter_coefficient, hhh the cell length scale and LrefL_{ref}Lref​ the reference_length of the reference condition named by reference in initialisation. KKK defaults to 5.0. A larger value treats more of the flow as smooth, and so limits less. The cube falls faster than the O(h2)O(h^2)O(h2) reconstruction error, so under refinement the limiter switches itself off in smooth regions and formal order is kept. barth jespersen has no such switch and limits wherever the bound binds; the one-dimensional limiters ignore limiter_coefficient.

Spatial order and the flux scheme

The limiter acts only at second order. order chooses between a first-order and a second-order reconstruction, and inviscid_flux_scheme chooses the approximate Riemann solver that turns the reconstructed face states into a flux.

Freezing the limiter

If a few cells whose limiter switches between iterations hold the residual at a noise floor, freeze the limiter with freeze_limiter_cycle. With a cell-based limiter the values are held per cell rather than per face. See Limiter switching for when the freeze applies under each kind of time marching.
